Peer Review

Peer Review Process

A transparent framework for the scholarly assessment of manuscripts from receipt to final decision.

Operational policy: Eligible research and review manuscripts are normally sent to at least two qualified reviewers and are evaluated under a double-anonymous (double-blind) review model.

Double-Blind Peer Review Process

Purpose of peer review

The purpose of scholarly peer review is to help evaluate the quality, validity, originality and significance of the research and to provide specialist feedback for improving the manuscript. Review should not be limited to language editing; it should focus on scientific argument, method, evidence, analysis and the contribution of the research.

Review stages

1. Receipt and registration

The manuscript and accompanying information are received and registered for initial examination. Completeness of files and author information is also checked at this stage.

2. Initial screening

Subject suitability, structure, compliance with author guidelines, apparent originality and obvious research-ethics issues are examined.

3. Reviewer selection

For eligible manuscripts, one or more reviewers with relevant expertise are selected. Potential conflicts of interest must be considered before a reviewer accepts the assignment.

4. Specialist evaluation

Reviewers provide specialist reports on scholarly quality, methodology, argument, evidence, sources and the contribution of the article.

5. Revision and re-assessment

Where necessary, the author submits a revised version and a clear response to the reviewers’ points. The manuscript may be sent for further review.

6. Final decision

The decision to accept, request revision or reject is made on the basis of scholarly evaluation and the journal’s policies. A request for revision does not guarantee acceptance.

Main evaluation criteria

  • Fit of the topic with the journal’s aims and scope
  • Originality and significance of the research problem
  • Clarity of the research question or aim
  • Strength of the theoretical and methodological framework
  • Validity of the data, evidence and method of analysis
  • Logical coherence and scholarly structure of the article
  • Accuracy and adequacy of sources and citations
  • Clarity of the conclusion and its connection to the findings
  • Scholarly contribution and added value of the research

Reviewer responsibilities

Reviewers must examine the manuscript confidentially, refrain from personal use of the information it contains, declare any potential conflict of interest, avoid personal or irrelevant remarks, and present their criticism in a documented, clear and constructive manner. Reviewers should also not request unnecessary citations intended to increase personal or journal citation counts.

Double-blind peer review

The journal uses a double-blind peer-review model. The identity of each author is concealed from reviewers, and reviewers’ identities are concealed from authors. The editor and authorized editorial staff may access identifying information as required for their duties and oversee the proper conduct of the process.

Keeping authors anonymous

Authors must remove their names, institutional affiliations, email addresses and other identifying details from the main manuscript file. Author details, acknowledgements, funding information and similar material should be supplied in a separate author-information file or in the designated submission fields. References to the authors’ previous work should be worded, where feasible, so as not to reveal their identity, while preserving scholarly integrity and accurate citation.

Reviewer anonymity and confidentiality

Reviewers’ names are not disclosed in reports sent to authors. Manuscripts, review reports and related correspondence are confidential and must not be disclosed outside the authorized review process or used for personal purposes. Eligible manuscripts are normally sent to at least two independent reviewers with relevant expertise. Reviewers must disclose potential conflicts of interest and assess manuscripts fairly, without bias and according to scholarly criteria.

The final decision to accept, request revision or reject a manuscript rests with the editor and authorized editorial staff, taking into account the review reports, scholarly quality and journal policies. A revised manuscript may be sent for further assessment. Identifying information may be disclosed only where required by applicable law or the journal’s formally announced policy.

Author’s right to revise

When revisions are requested, the author should clearly indicate the changes made and, where there is a scientific disagreement, provide a reasoned response. A difference of opinion between author and reviewer does not prevent a scholarly reply; the final decision rests with the editorial office.

Appeals against decisions

Appeals must be documented, respectful and limited to scholarly issues or identifiable errors in the evaluation process. An appeal does not in itself mean acceptance of the article or an obligation to change the decision, and will be considered within the journal’s official policies.

Important note on review times

The journal refrains from announcing unrealistic timescales or guaranteeing acceptance. The duration of review depends on the subject of the article, the availability of specialist reviewers, the need for revision and the number of evaluation rounds. After formal publication begins and real data become available, reportable time indicators will be published on the Journal Metrics page.
